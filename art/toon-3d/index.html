Sprite rig or toon 3D?

2D sprite rig (current prototype)Toon-shaded 3D (this page)
Camera anglesSide view only. Every new angle means redrawing every part.Any angle for free. Cinematic submissions, replays, walkouts.
Grappling layeringHand-written draw order per position (which leg is in front of whom).Solved by the depth buffer. Bodies can wrap around each other.
Authoring holdsAngles per bone, checked by eye.Contact points: "right hand on Blue's neck", "feet hooked in Blue's thighs". IK does the rest.
Gear swapsSwap a drawing per slot.Swap a mesh or texture per slot. Same idea.
Art pipelineCheapest: flat drawings, any illustrator.Needs a 3D modeler (Blender) and a toon shader. Base animations can come from motion capture.
Browser costTiny. Runs anywhere.WebGL with a few thousand triangles per fighter. Fine on phones from the last several years.
  • What makes it read as 2D: stepped cel shading (three flat tones), ink outlines drawn by an inflated back-face shell, a long lens that flattens perspective in the game view, and animation held at 12 frames per second while the camera moves smoothly.
  • How holds are built: each pose in poses.json places the hips and spine, then gives every hand and foot a target, often on the other fighter's body. Two-bone IK bends the elbows and knees to reach it. Squeeze blends a loose and a tight version of the hold.
  • These fighters are placeholders made from simple shapes so the rig and camera can be judged. A real model would be sculpted in Blender, rigged to the same joints, and shaded the same way.
